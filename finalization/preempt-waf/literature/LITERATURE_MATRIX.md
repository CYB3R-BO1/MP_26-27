## Novelty boundary

The defensible claim is not “the first system to predict attacks before exploitation,” “the first predictive WAF,” or “the first adaptive WAF.” PreRCE-Fuse already studies strictly pre-RCE forecasting on the same eight AIT testbeds, while prior adaptive/cost-sensitive defense work covers risk-conditioned response.

A narrower candidate question remains: **Does HTTP request-sequence evidence add calibrated, campaign-general predictive value over NetFlow and simple rate/error heuristics, and can a feedback-aware controller convert that value into lower independently verified exploit success at a measured legitimate-user cost?** This is credible only after new independent campaigns/applications and outcome-instrumented intervention experiments. It is a proposed distinction, not a demonstrated novelty result.

## Search limitations

The review prioritized the named 2026 works, their official artifacts, forward/backward terminology searches, and the unexpectedly close PreRCE-Fuse paper. It is not a systematic review. DawnGuard could not be uniquely identified from the supplied name and was not used as evidence. Publication metadata and artifacts can change; repeat the search immediately before proposal submission.
